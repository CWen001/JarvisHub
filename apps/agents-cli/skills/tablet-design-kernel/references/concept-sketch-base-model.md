# Tablet Concept Sketch BaseModel

Version: `2.0.0`

This BaseModel is the mandatory professional expression model for one tablet-computer Concept Sketch. It adapts the accepted four-authority `TabletConceptV2` semantics into a provider-ready visual instruction without creating a persisted Tablet Concept object or a second runtime authority.

## Frame invariant

- One continuous image frame.
- Exactly one unmistakable, complete, bare-tablet product Hero.
- Zero or one subordinate evidence item only when it is necessary to explain the accepted form or state.
- Low information density: no story sequence, equal-weight board, exhaustive detail grid, dimensions, parameter table, portrait-led scene, or text-heavy presentation.
- The tablet—not a person, room, accessory, interface, or graphic layout—is the absolute visual subject.

## Design Identity

State one irreducible whole-product proposition and translate it into visible form. Define:

- the product's central idea;
- the perceptible whole-product character;
- one primary signature outcome;
- one bounded Leading Departure, unless an explicitly conservative brief forbids departure;
- 1–3 Identity Boundaries that prevent the concept from becoming visually fragmented, futuristic, decorative, or over-developed.

The Leading Departure must be a coordinated product resolution, not an added feature or isolated styling gesture.

## Portfolio Position and Maturity Anchor

Specify a brand-neutral market tier, portability class, owned product form, dominant task, and at most two secondary tasks. Preserve a concise Maturity Anchor:

- the current category expectation that keeps the product credible;
- the mature principles retained in proportion, enclosure coherence, display hierarchy, handling, construction expression, and detail restraint;
- no imitation of a named released product, logo, signature geometry, or proprietary visual language.

## Product elements and relationships

Name only elements whose form or physical relationship this concept actually owns: bare enclosure, display/glass, controls/openings, camera or sensor nodes, and any concept-defining pen, keyboard, support, cover, or carrying element.

For each visible relationship, state the perceptible result: alignment, attachment, separation, closure, support, protection, load path, or transition. Do not model internal BOM parts or generic third-party accessories.

## Hero State and carry relationship

Define one complete Hero State with:

- orientation and purpose;
- support or holding condition;
- human or surface contact relationship;
- perceptible load and weight distribution;
- primary input;
- whole-product character in that state.

Also state the typical carry relationship. Hero and carry may be the same state. Add another state only when support, attachment, load, contact, closure, or primary input materially changes; angle alone is not another state.

## Dimensional intent and proportions

Express scale and geometry qualitatively unless the user or a verified source supplied exact values. Resolve:

- whole-product aspect and silhouette;
- display-to-enclosure relationship;
- visual thickness through continuous section logic rather than knife-edge deception;
- edge radius and corner continuity;
- weight and visual-mass distribution;
- working geometry in the Hero State;
- accessory thickness or footprint when applicable.

Never invent millimetres, weight, tolerances, battery volume, hinge range, or structural performance.

## Display, glass, and front hierarchy

Define display boundary, cover-glass relationship, bezel rhythm, corner relationship, sensor/camera placement, primary orientation, and inactive/active interface state when visible. The front must balance immersion, grasping allowance, node accommodation, and directionality rather than erasing every border.

Use at most one quiet, plausible interface state. The interface supports product identity; it does not become the Hero.

## Bare enclosure, rear surface, and product nodes

The authored enclosure must remain independently legible and complete. A protective case, folio, wraparound shell, perimeter/corner case, or third-party-looking cover may not substitute for it.

Resolve:

- back-panel main surface and hierarchy;
- camera, connector, antenna, speaker, and service nodes as a small ordered family;
- controlled protrusion and stable table contact where applicable;
- consistent edge sections and transitions;
- necessary seams and openings aligned to a limited structural order.

Do not fake a seamless object by deleting every credible construction boundary.

## Human contact, handling, and carrying

Specify the frequent grip edge or contact zones, corner pressure, tactile transition, friction, balance, and carry closure. Handling evidence must be visible or tactile in the product design, not merely described by a hand or lifestyle scene.

If a stylus, keyboard, support, or state-defining companion exists, resolve its use position, return path, protected carry condition, alignment, and effect on thickness, footprint, balance, and identity. Do not add an accessory without a product-defining role.

## Input, controls, and feedback

Resolve touch, pen, key, physical control, connector, speaker, and feedback locations relevant to the accepted direction. Feedback belongs near the action, uses one restrained hierarchy, and cannot claim unverified software or hardware behavior.

## Integrated Design Resolutions

For every important design move, make the following visible:

- the coherent product problem;
- the chosen coordinated answer;
- affected product elements and states;
- 1–4 perceptible visual or tactile outcomes;
- the accepted local and whole-product trade-off.

At least one resolution carries the signature outcome. Other resolutions remain subordinate and reinforce the same identity.

## Concrete CMF and touch

Define a specific, restrained relationship between:

- base material and color family;
- main-surface roughness or gloss;
- edge/contact-zone finish;
- display/glass reflection;
- node and seam treatment;
- tactile evidence and wear/cleaning trade-off.

CMF must belong to declared elements and states. Do not use a generic premium palette, high-saturation decoration, or unsupported exotic material claim.

## Process-boundary honesty

Turn necessary seams, ports, speakers, antenna breaks, camera protection, assembly edges, and maintenance boundaries into ordered details. Align and consolidate them where credible, while avoiding invented tolerances, internal mechanisms, waterproofing, durability, thermal, or manufacturing claims.

## Visual thesis and evidence priority

The frame must communicate, in order:

1. complete whole-product silhouette and central idea;
2. primary signature outcome / Leading Departure;
3. Hero State and key physical relationships;
4. material, edge, node, and process precision;
5. zero or one necessary subordinate clue.

Anything that competes with these priorities must be removed.

## Camera, lighting, and environment

Choose one view that best proves the accepted design—normally a refined three-quarter front or rear product view at believable eye level. Use controlled studio or restrained contextual lighting, continuous edge highlights, readable dark/light separation, realistic contact shadow, and enough negative space to inspect the complete product.

The environment may establish scale or support, but must remain quiet. Avoid dramatic fog, excessive bloom, impossible reflections, floating parts, cinematic scenery, and backgrounds that hide the silhouette.

## Forbidden visual outcomes

Unless a later explicitly requested Artifact requires them, forbid:

- multiple equal-weight variants or multiple complete tablets;
- contact sheets, six-view boards, exploded views, storyboards, diagrams, parameter tables, callout text, or marketing copy;
- a person, hand, portrait, room, or accessory becoming the primary subject;
- cropped product edges or an incomplete enclosure;
- a case or folio masquerading as the designed tablet body;
- unsupported exact dimensions, specifications, engineering validation, brand marks, or released-product claims;
- feature accumulation, decorative segmentation, random grooves, gratuitous LEDs, generic sci-fi surfacing, or unrelated novelty;
- contradictory states, impossible support, unexplained floating accessories, unstable table contact, or incoherent weight distribution;
- generic slabs whose Prompt contains adjectives but no perceptible design evidence.
